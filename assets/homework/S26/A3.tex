\prob{P4}  \emph{Chapter 2 defines \emph{equicontinuous} and proves the Ascoli-Arzel\`a Theorem.  However, it gives no related exercises!  Here is one.}

\epart{a}  Consider $V=C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.  For $\mu,\alpha \in (0,1)$ let
	$$f_{\alpha,\mu}(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 \le x \le \alpha \\
	                                    (x-\alpha)/\mu, & \alpha \le x \le \alpha + \mu \\
	                                    1, & \alpha + \mu \le x \le 1 \end{cases}$$
Sketch the graphs of $f_{\alpha,\mu}$ in three cases of your choice.  (\emph{Generally $\mu$ should be small.})  Explain why $f_{\alpha,\mu}\in V$.  Let
	$$E = \left\{f_{\alpha,\mu}\,:\,\mu,\alpha \in (0,1)\right\} \subset V.$$
Show that $E$ is \emph{not} equicontinuous.

\epart{b}  Now define
	$$g_{\alpha,\mu}(x) = \int_0^x f_{\alpha,\mu}(t)\,dt,$$
and let $H = \{g_{\alpha,\mu}\,:\,\mu,\alpha \in (0,1)\}$.  Sketch the graphs of $g_{\alpha,\mu}$ in the same three cases as in part \textbf{(a)}.  Then show that $H$ \emph{is} an equicontinuous subset of $V$.
